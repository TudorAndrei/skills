# Exercises for breaking sameness

Use when the work stops improving, the output sounds like everything before it, or the user cannot start. Run each without expectation: the goal is fresh excitement, not a better result. Adapt the parameters to the medium; invent new ones freely.

## When blocked

- **Small steps**: commit to one tiny unit a day (one line, one sketch, one function). Quality does not matter; the channel reopens.
- **Distraction on purpose**: hold the problem loosely and do something on autopilot (walk, drive, shower, dishes). The busy conscious mind frees the rest.
- **Surrender the outline**: write with no map and see where the premise goes.
- **Random input**: open a book at a random line, draw a card, throw coins; read the result as a clue for the current problem.
- **First-thing capture**: write immediately after waking, before moving; or do five minutes of hard physical effort and then write five pages without steering.

## When the work sounds the same

- **Change the environment**: dark room, different time of day, different position, different tool (laptop → paper, pro camera → phone, dominant hand → other hand).
- **Change the stakes**: pretend it is the last take ever, or pretend it is only rehearsal.
- **Invite an audience**: one silent observer changes how most people work.
- **Change the context**: give the piece a new meaning or backstory without changing its content (a love song sung to someone else entirely).
- **Force a perspective**: set conditions that naturally push the behaviour you want (headphones very loud → the player goes quiet).
- **Write for someone else**: make the piece your favourite artist would ask you for; then try an artist opposite to your style.
- **Add imagery**: describe a vivid scene and make the piece "like that" instead of giving a technical reference.
- **Limit the information**: give collaborators the barest sketch, not the demo, so they bring themselves.
- **Vary inputs**: film with sound off, one song on repeat, only the first word of each sentence.
- **Scrap the palette**: start the next project without your usual colours, chords, stack or structure. Skills stay; habits go.
- **Try the opposite**: for each rule the work follows, test its inverse, or take it to the extreme.
- **What-if questions**: remove every adverb; make every loud part quiet; treat this as the first painting anyone will ever see.
