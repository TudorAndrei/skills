# Collaboration and feedback

## Cooperation, not competition

- The best idea wins, whoever owns it. Preferring an idea because it is yours is inexperience.
- **Choice C**: when one person wants A and another wants B, pick neither. Keep working until a C appears that both find better. It may mix A and B or contain neither. Settling for the sake of progress loses for everyone.
- Keep going until everyone loves the work. If one person loves it and another does not, there is an unsolved problem; the work has not gone far enough.
- Contrast is useful: two identical filters make no new colour. Friction between different views is fuel, as long as nobody needs it their way. A single-leader model also works when everyone agrees to serve that one vision.
- **First, do no harm**: when joining someone else's project, protect the magic in its rough state. Sometimes the best contribution is to change nothing.
- Demonstrate ideas instead of arguing them. The person with the idea shows it or supervises it until it matches what they meant.

## Giving feedback

- Talk about the work, never the person.
- Be specific and clinical: "these two colours clash in this area", not "I don't like the colours".
- Hold back your fix; the maker may find a better one.

## Receiving feedback

- Listen to understand the person; their notes reveal their own view.
- Repeat the note back; what you heard is often not what they said, and what they said is often not what they meant. Ask questions until the difference is clear.
- A specific note on one detail is not a verdict on the whole work.
- Read a suggested fix as a pointer to a real issue, then look at that area in the context of the whole piece.
- If a note stings, step away and come back neutral. You may agree, or double down on the first instinct.
- You are the only audience that must love the work.

## Listening

Listen without preparing a reply, judging or defending. Suspend disbelief until you fully understand. Most people are rarely fully heard; full attention changes what they say.
